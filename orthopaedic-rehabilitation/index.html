Orthopaedic Physiotherapy

What orthopaedic physiotherapy treats

Orthopaedic physiotherapy deals with pain and lost movement coming from the musculoskeletal system — bones, joints, discs, muscles and tendons — whether the problem arrived slowly with age, suddenly with an injury, or as part of recovering from an operation.

This is that department at Navthera in Jaipur: the back gone into spasm, the knee that will no longer manage stairs, the shoulder that has stopped reaching overhead. Treatment begins from two questions — what is producing the pain, and what has it stopped you doing? The aim is to settle symptoms enough that movement becomes possible, then rebuild the strength and range that pain and rest take away.

How rehabilitation helps, by condition

Slip disc and sciatica

When a disc presses on a nerve root, the pain does not stay in the back — it runs into the buttock and down the leg, often with numbness or tingling, and sitting makes it worse. Rehabilitation works to ease that pressure, give the spine back the movement it has been guarding, and strengthen the hips and trunk around it. Robotic decompression applies controlled, computer-guided traction alongside that exercise work.

Back and neck pain, spondylosis, posture

Spondylosis is wear in the discs and small joints of the spine: stiffness first thing, pain that builds through a long day at a desk, sometimes headaches that start in the neck. Rehabilitation works to free the segments that have stiffened, retrain the deep muscles that hold your head and lower back steady, and change how you sit and lift. TECAR, radiofrequency that warms tissue at depth, can settle a guarded area beforehand.

Knee and hip pain, and osteoarthritis

Worn knee and hip joints hurt most under load — stairs, getting out of a chair, the first steps of the day — and the muscles around them weaken as you move less. Rehabilitation rebuilds thigh and hip strength, works back the range the joint has lost, and retrains walking. When exercise on land is still too sore, the aquatherapy pool lets you work with buoyancy taking load off the joint.

Shoulder, elbow, wrist and heel pain

Tendon and shoulder problems follow a pattern: pain on one particular movement, pain at night, and reach or grip quietly disappearing. Rehabilitation loads the tendon in careful stages so it tolerates work again, frees a shoulder that has tightened, and corrects the shoulder-blade control behind much of it. Shockwave, pressure waves used for long-standing tendon problems, may be part of that; sports injuries may suit sports rehabilitation.

Recovery after surgery

After a knee or hip replacement, a ligament reconstruction or fracture fixation, the joint is swollen and stiff and the muscles around it stop firing properly. Rehabilitation follows your surgeon's protocol — settling swelling, recovering safe range, then loading the limb again through standing, stairs and walking. Cryotherapy uses controlled cold for pain and swelling, and FES, electrical stimulation, helps switch a quiet quadriceps back on.

Stiffness after immobilisation, and long-standing pain

Time in a cast or sling leaves a joint stiff and the muscle around it wasted, and long-standing pain leaves the whole body deconditioned and wary of moving. Rehabilitation works range back into the joint, rebuilds the strength that disuse takes, and steps activity up at a pace your body accepts. LASER, a light-based therapy for pain and healing, may be used; where falls and frailty dominate, geriatric physiotherapy may fit better.
